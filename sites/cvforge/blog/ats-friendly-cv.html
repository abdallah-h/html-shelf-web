How to Make Your CV ATS-Friendly: A Practical Checklist

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-01

Most medium and large employers collect applications through an applicant tracking system (ATS) — software such as Workday, Greenhouse, Lever or Taleo. The ATS reads your CV, extracts details like your job titles and dates, and lets recruiters search and filter candidates.

A myth worth clearing up

You may have read that "75% of CVs are rejected by robots before a human sees them." That figure is widely repeated but has no reliable source. In practice, most systems store and organise applications, and recruiters decide who moves forward — often by searching for keywords or using knockout questions on the application form. So the real goals are simple: make sure the software reads your CV correctly, and make sure it contains the words a recruiter will search for.

The ATS-friendly checklist

Layout

  • Use a simple, single-column layout for online applications. Complex multi-column designs, tables and text boxes can be parsed out of order.
  • Don't put important information in the page header or footer — some parsers skip them. Keep your name and contact details in the main body.
  • Never put text inside images, including skill bars or icons. Software can't read them.

Headings and dates

  • Use standard section names: "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Certifications". Creative headings like "My Journey" confuse parsers.
  • Write dates in a consistent format (e.g. "Mar. 2022 – Present") for every role.
  • Put the job title and company clearly on their own lines.

Keywords

  • Read the job description and note the skills, tools and qualifications it repeats.
  • Use those exact terms where they truthfully apply — in your summary, skills and experience bullets.
  • Write abbreviations both ways once: "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)".
  • Don't stuff keywords or hide white text. Recruiters read the CV too, and it looks dishonest.

File type

  • Follow the instructions in the posting. If it asks for Word, send .docx.
  • If no format is specified, a text-based PDF (one you can highlight and copy text from — not a scanned image) is read well by modern systems.
  • Use a simple file name: Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf.

Test your CV in 30 seconds

Open your PDF, select all the text and paste it into a plain text editor. If the result is in a sensible order with nothing missing, an ATS will read it fine. If sections are jumbled or text is missing, simplify your layout.
