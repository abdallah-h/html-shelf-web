CV vs Resume: What's the Difference (and Which One Do You Need?)

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-01

"CV" and "resume" are often used as if they mean the same thing — and sometimes they do. Whether there is a real difference depends mostly on where you are applying and what kind of job it is.

The short answer

  • In the United States and Canada, a resume is the short document used for most jobs, while a CV is a long, detailed record used mainly in academia, research and medicine.
  • In the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and across much of Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia, "CV" is simply the everyday name for the job-application document — the same thing Americans call a resume.

So if a job in Cairo, Dubai or London asks for your CV, they almost always mean a concise one-to-two-page document — not a twenty-page academic record.

What goes in an academic CV

An academic CV lists everything relevant to a scholarly career: degrees and thesis titles, publications, research experience, teaching, grants and funding, conference presentations, awards and professional memberships. It grows throughout your career and is rarely trimmed.

Personal details differ by country too

Conventions about photos and personal information vary widely:

  • In the US and UK, photos, date of birth and marital status are usually left out, partly because of anti-discrimination practices.
  • In parts of continental Europe and many Middle Eastern countries, including a professional photo — and sometimes nationality — is common and often expected.

When in doubt, follow the job posting and look at examples from people hired in that country.

Which one should you write?

  1. Use the word the job ad uses, and match the expected length.
  2. For almost every non-academic job, prepare a focused one-to-two-page document tailored to the role.
  3. Only prepare a full academic CV if you're applying for teaching, research or fellowship positions.

Many people keep both: a long "master" version listing everything, and a short tailored version they adapt for each application.
