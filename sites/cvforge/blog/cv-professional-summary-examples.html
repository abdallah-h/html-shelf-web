How to Write a CV Professional Summary (With 6 Examples)

2 min read · Updated 2026-10-01

Your professional summary is the short paragraph at the top of your CV. It's often the first thing a recruiter reads, so its job is simple: show in a few seconds that you fit the role and make them want to read the rest.

The four-part formula

  1. Who you are — your title or field and years of experience
  2. What you're strong at — two or three specialties relevant to the job
  3. Proof — one achievement or result
  4. What you want — the kind of role or value you'll bring (optional)

Keep it to 2–4 sentences and roughly 30–60 words.

Six examples

Fresh graduate

Business Administration graduate with internship experience in marketing analytics and social media campaigns. Skilled in Excel, Google Analytics and content planning, with a graduation project that grew a student brand's Instagram following by 3,000 in one semester. Seeking an entry-level marketing role.

Career changer

Former secondary-school teacher moving into instructional design, bringing eight years of experience creating engaging lessons for diverse learners. Recently completed a certificate in e-learning development and built three interactive courses in Articulate Storyline.

Software developer

Full-stack developer with 5 years of experience building web applications in C#, .NET and JavaScript. Delivered ERP integrations and custom reporting for 20+ business clients, reducing manual data entry by up to 60%. Looking to grow into a technical lead role.

Sales

B2B sales executive with 6 years in the software industry and a consistent record of exceeding targets, including 128% of quota in 2024. Strong at building long-term client relationships and managing pipelines in Salesforce.

Accounting

Chartered accountant with 7 years of experience in month-end close, financial reporting and audit preparation for manufacturing companies. Cut the monthly close from ten days to six by standardising reconciliations in the ERP system.

Customer service

Customer service specialist with 4 years in telecom support, handling 60+ calls and tickets a day while maintaining a 95% satisfaction rating. Fluent in Arabic and English and experienced in training new team members.

Summary or objective?

An objective focuses on what you want; a summary focuses on what you offer. Employers care more about the second, so most CVs today use a summary. If you're a graduate or changing careers, you can end with one short line about the role you're seeking — as in the examples above.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Clichés with no proof: "hard-working team player with excellent communication skills." Show it with a fact instead.
  • Too long: if it's more than four sentences, it's turning into a cover letter.
  • Not tailored: reuse the job ad's key terms where they're true for you.
  • Mixing styles: you can write with or without "I", but stay consistent across the CV.
